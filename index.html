What building these involves

The engineering

A dataset listing shows the output. These are the stages that produce it.

Ingestion and indexing. Raw studies sit in hospital systems in proprietary vendor formats, spread across archives that were never built to be queried as a corpus. The first task is knowing what exists: cataloging every recording, reconciling it against the clinical record, and pulling it on a schedule. I run the daily electroencephalography pipeline for Beth Israel Deaconess, from indexing through archiving.

Patient matching. A physiological recording is only useful if it can be tied to the right patient across systems that identify people differently. Getting this wrong is worse than useless, because it silently corrupts every downstream analysis.

Format conversion and annotation. Vendor formats differ by manufacturer and by decade. EEG recordings are standardized using the Brain Imaging Data Structure (BIDS); polysomnography recordings are likewise standardized using BIDS; ECG recordings are converted to WFDB format; and clinical imaging studies are converted from DICOM to NIfTI and organized according to BIDS. I generate the BIDS-compliant annotation files for EEGs processed through the platform’s conversion pipeline.

Electronic health record extraction and linkage. A waveform without clinical context answers few questions. I built the daily extraction of Epic-sourced record data through an enterprise data warehouse, and the linkage of that clinical data to the corresponding physiological recordings.

De-identification. Clinical recordings and the notes that accompany them can contain protected health information. Assembling records from Mass General, Beth Israel Deaconess, Brigham and Women's, Boston Children's, Emory and Stanford involves institution-specific approvals and data-use arrangements at each site, which is slow enough to deter many multi-site studies. Removing identifiers is one part of what makes a shared resource possible; the rest is governance. I extended Philter, the UCSF natural-language de-identification tool, with a cross-institutional abstraction layer, and applied it to millions of clinical notes, imaging reports and neurology reports. Running the tool is not by itself a compliance determination: each contributing institution reviews and approves its own release, and the resources are distributed under the governed-access terms described below rather than renegotiated study by study.

Harmonization. Each contributing site records under its own equipment, conventions and documentation practices. Making contributing institutions’ data comparable, rather than merely co-located, is done across institutional boundaries by definition.

Release and governed access. Each resource is registered under a DOI, versioned, and distributed through a credentialed-access layer that enforces every contributing institution's terms through signed data-use agreements. As of 31 August 2026, 1,016 researchers had been approved from 1,717 applications.

I am one of three engineers on the platform, and a named Data Coordinator on the Mass General Brigham institutional review board protocols under which this work is conducted.

Method: independence and counting rules

Method

A work counts as independent only if none of its authors is me or anyone I have co-authored with.

That is a stricter test than a simple check for my own name. Large clinical datasets are built by large teams, and papers using them often carry an author in common. A work is excluded if any of its authors appears among the 121 people who have co-authored a paper or dataset release with me, across 19 such works. Every citation in the table above was checked individually against that complete list. The same co-author screen is applied to every work labeled independent elsewhere on this page, and to the works underlying the independent-institution register.

Where a downstream model was trained, tuned, validated, benchmarked, or evaluated by a group I have co-authored with, it is labeled shared authorship rather than quietly counted. ECGFounder and MORGOTH are in that category; ECG-CPC, ECG-LFM, NeuroLex and CAMEL are not.

Relationship designations. Designations are assigned at the work level and describe what a work did, not what an institution decided. Institutions are taken from the author affiliations that work prints, and all eligible institutions represented on the same work inherit that work’s relationship to the underlying work or resource. An institution may display a stronger designation if a separate qualifying work documents a stronger form of engagement. Where more than one qualifying relationship exists, the displayed category reflects the strongest documented engagement in this order: downstream use; key or exemplary resource treatment; invited editorial; ordinary citation. Key resource is reserved for works that identify the resource as key, exemplary, recommended or field-significant; ordinary provenance or background references are classified as cited. The word landmark is not used as a category here, since few sources use it.

Counting institutions. Institution counts are based on distinct organizationally independent institutions named in the author affiliations of the qualifying works represented on this page, which include citations, preprints, documented uses and invited editorials. Internal departments, laboratories, centers and other subunits are folded into their parent institution. Separate incorporation is strong evidence of organizational independence; where status remains ambiguous, the conservative reading is used. Independent affiliations take many forms: universities, hospitals, research institutes, companies, foundations, professional societies and government organizations are all represented here.

Two caveats worth stating. Citation counts can understate use: the datasets are distributed through self-service credentialed access and can be used without generating a formal citation. And several works listed as preprints may publish later; status shown is current, not permanent.
